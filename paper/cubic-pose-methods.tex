\subsection{Higher-order pose remainder diagnostic}\label{sec:cubicpose}
The enclosing-domain calculation extends to Taylor degree $d$. Let $S_j$ be
the $L^2$ Frobenius norm of the known Fourier adjoint field's $j$th derivative
tensor over the enclosing domain. For the rigid pose path
$y(t)=\exp(t\Omega)x+t b$, use
$M_1=\operatorname{hypot}(a\sqrt{3}/2,s_t\|B_{\rm det}\|)$ and
$M_j=a^j\sqrt{3}/2$ for $j\ge2$, where $B_{\rm det}$ maps detector shifts to
object coordinates. The joint pose ball justifies the hypotenuse expression.
The chain rule, tensor Cauchy--Schwarz and volume preservation give
\[
 \left\|(f\circ y)^{(d+1)}\right\|_{L^2}
 \le \sum_{j=1}^{d+1}\mathcal B_{d+1,j}(M_1,\ldots,M_{d+1})S_j,
\]
with partial exponential Bell polynomials $\mathcal B$. Division by $(d+1)!$
bounds the Taylor remainder. The existing approximate-embedding phase residual
also needs a higher-order Leibniz pad. These are standard differentiation and
norm inequalities, not a new general statistical theorem.

A declared two-case feasibility probe retains every degree in
Table~\ref{tab:higherremainder}, at identical weights, acquisition geometry and
one-degree/0.5\,\AA\ joint pose budgets. Both use EMPIAR-10049 geometry and the
same $B=2,P=1$ density class. The values include multiplication by $B+P$.
They show a potential reduction in one bias component. They are \emph{not}
confidence-interval widths: replacing a remainder while omitting the extra
polynomial fields would be invalid. The first feature's degree-two enclosing
remainder is 3.550 of its total bias bound 5.155; its sign power remains
negligible. No degree was selected by comparing resulting coverage.
The Gaussian sigmas are respectively 20 and 10\,\AA; both use frequency radius
12.

\paragraph{Full cubic extension.}
A separate bounded diagnostic retains all cubic fields for the first case;
its protocol and code precede its empirical outcome. Write the nonlinear phase
as $\Phi_0+t\Phi_1+t^2\Phi_2+t^3\Phi_3+\cdots$. The third exponential
coefficient is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_3&=i\Phi_3-\Phi_1\Phi_2-i\Phi_1^3/6,\\
 \Phi_3&=-a^2\|u_{\rm rot}\|^2\Phi_{1,\rm rot}/6.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last identity uses $[u]_\times^3=-\|u\|^2[u]_\times$; dropping it would
omit rotation curvature. For degree $m\in\{1,2,3\}$, the normalized symmetric
lift $z_\alpha=\sqrt{m!/\alpha!}\,u^\alpha$, $|\alpha|=m$, has
$\|z\|^2=\|u\|^{2m}\le1$. Thus there are $5+15+35=55$ columns per particle,
using twenty spatial monomials. Positive degree/particle scales $d_{im}$ give
a joint lifted radius $L=\sqrt{\sum_{im}d_{im}}$. Cheap quadrature chooses
these scales only; order-80 integration and a degree-six product-kernel pad
protect the continuous spectral audit. Four fresh Gaussian probes and forty
power steps use failure probability $10^{-6}/12$.

For the pilot-corrected estimator in Equation~\eqref{eq:estimator},
$\widehat\theta=\ip{\ell}{\rho_0}+w^\top(y-A_0\rho_0)$,
the residual cross-term argument remains valid with this larger field operator.
For $H\ge\|\ell-A_0^*w\|$, $c\ge\|F^*(\ell-A_0^*w)\|$,
$f\ge\sup_{\|v\|\le L}\|Fv\|$, $p\ge\|F^*\rho_0\|$ and quartic field
remainder $r$, the conditional bias bound is
\[
 B\sqrt{H^2+2Lc+f^2}+\min(Pf,Lp)+(B+P)r.
\]
Its proof expands the squared residual norm and applies Cauchy--Schwarz to the
known pilot. Twenty continuous cell and Gaussian moments evaluate the pairings.
Independent scalar phase recurrences, direct quadrature, adjoint identities and
the old quadratic restriction check signs and normalization. The quadratic and cubic intervals are alternatives; their unadjusted minimum
is not a joint confidence claim. The completed audit takes 2,187 seconds and 1.42 GB peak resident memory.
Its total bias bound is 2.467, comprising a 0.815 quartic remainder and
1.652 from the polynomial/density terms. Noise adds 0.601 to the half-width.
The relative width falls from 0.473 for the quadratic enclosing-domain audit
to 0.252, but minimum reference sign power is only
$1.48\times10^{-10}$. Thus retaining cubic terms improves the bound without
making this feature detectable under the assumed class. Merely removing the
remainder from the current expression would still leave a half-width above
the reference feature magnitude; that counterfactual is not a valid interval
or an impossibility result for a different estimator.

Follow-up numerical checks evaluate all 28,160 actual-frequency Gaussian moments
against independent order-200 quadrature (maximum per-column relative norm
error $2.35\times10^{-14}$), reproduce the fixed-pose residual bias, and verify
an actual-operator adjoint pairing. Selected continuous block-norm/design-scale
ratios are 0.982--1.029; no scale is changed after the probes. These checks do
not validate all floating-point errors. A stricter factor-twenty toy remainder
slack test fails at 35.57, which remains a conservatism finding; separate
nonlinear tests verify fourth-order error scaling. Neither these checks nor the
reduced interval calibrate experimental pose radii.

\paragraph{Pose-aware cubic weight design.}
The failed fixed-weight power result motivates optimizing the weights themselves.
A separate single-case development protocol, committed before fitting, retains
its target, class and pose budget, uses the inherited positive scales, and limits
L-BFGS-B to 30 iterations with a requested 40 evaluations. Every evaluation and
improving checkpoint is retained. Three approximate Ritz modes and a
log-sum-exp smoothing of 0.002 guide optimization. A fresh four-probe,
forty-step audit follows weight selection. Both design and final spectral audit
use order 64, while the nominal Gram uses order 80. This case is running;
no empirical optimized width or usefulness result is asserted here.

Let $G=A_0A_0^*$, $a=A_0\ell$, and let $G_Q$ be its quadrature approximation.
If $E$ is the kernel-error bound and $t$ the transfer vector, set
$\eta=E\|t\|^2$ and
\[
 H(w)^2=\|\ell\|^2-2a^\top w+w^\top(G_Q+\eta I)w.
\]
The bound $|w^\top(G_Q-G)w|\le E(\sum_j|t_j|b_j(w))^2$
and Cauchy--Schwarz imply $G_Q+\eta I\succeq G$, where $b_j$ is the
real/imaginary pair amplitude. Hence $H$ is a convex affine-norm majorant of
the continuous residual. With nonnegative coefficient-mass matrix $M$ and
degree-six kernel pad $E_6$, define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 J(w)={}&BL\sqrt{\|F_Q(w)\|_{\rm op}^2+E_6\|Mb(w)\|^2}\\
       &+L\|F_{\rm cont}(w)^*\rho_0\|+(B+P)r_{\rm PSD}(w).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $r_{\rm PSD}$ is the Bell-weighted sum of real derivative-Gram norms,
with the approximate-embedding residual pad. Each term is convex and positively
homogeneous. Thus $z\|w\|+BH(w)+J(w)$ is a convex sum-width majorant.
The derivative-Gram diagonal safeguards and final cancellation guard remain
ordinary floating-point precautions, not validated numerical enclosures.

\begin{samepage}
\begin{proposition}[Continuous lower certificate for cubic design]
Suppose $g_J^\top w'\le J(w')$ for all $w'$, $\bar H\ge\|\ell-A_0^*w\|>0$,
and $e_G\ge\|(G_Q-G)w\|$. Set
\[
\begin{aligned}
 f&=B/\bar H,\\
 d&=\|f(a-G_Qw)-g_J\|+fe_G,\\
 c&=\min(1,z/d).
\end{aligned}
\]
Then $\max\{0,cf(\|\ell\|^2-a^\top w)\}$ lower-bounds the infimum of the
sum-width majorant. Use $c=1$ if $d=0$.
\end{proposition}
\end{samepage}
\begin{proof}
Take $v=f(\ell-A_0^*w)$, so $\|v\|\le B$ and
$\|A_0(cv)-cg_J\|\le z$. Norm duality and the supporting inequality imply
$z\|w'\|+B\|\ell-A_0^*w'\|+J(w')\ge\ip{cv}{\ell}$ for every $w'$.
Replacing the continuous residual by its majorant preserves the lower bound.
The zero lower bound is also valid.
\end{proof}
The dual uses $a-G_Qw$, without $\eta w$, because its density direction is the
actual continuous residual. Any unit spatial directions supply spectral-norm
supports, including inaccurate or partial Ritz modes. Convex mixtures remain
supports; combining their gradient with the coefficient-mass gradient by the
normalized two-component norm inequality preserves this property even with
positive smoothing. Entropy means support equality at the candidate is not
asserted. A Ritz value is never used as an upper certificate. The reported gap
compares the unsmoothed, order-matched triangle objective; a later joint-cross
or folded-normal refinement does not inherit that gap.

An independently assembled four-coordinate conic problem gives optimum
2.071569215466, audited primal 2.071569215459 and continuous dual
2.071569195636. Their agreement is a numerical implementation check, not a
cryo-EM calibration result. The original conic test returned
\texttt{optimal\_inaccurate} at a provable zero-weight optimum; that failed
attempt is retained, and a separate analytic boundary check now covers it.
Directional gradients, convex supports and partial-mode bookkeeping are also
checked before the empirical fit.

A subsequent eight-coordinate, two-particle conic check uses unequal geometry
and nuisance budgets. SCS gives objective 1.998226582036, with an audited
relative gap $4.54\times10^{-7}$; a preceding CLARABEL failure on the same
geometry is retained. Random spatial-mode supports and empty-eigensolver failure
paths are checked separately. A fresh rerun of the zero-weight boundary gives
2.528968168770 versus analytic optimum 2.528968168766 while retaining the
\texttt{optimal\_inaccurate} warning. Final-weight two-tolerance operator
checks are declared and pending; none of these tests validates all NUFFT error.

A separate block-plus-low-rank coordinate metric includes noise, nominal-density
and quartic-penalty curvature, while omitting the shared spectral and pilot
curvature. Its nonsymmetric forward map requires the transpose map for gradients.
A compute-only probe at the original weights verifies whitening to
$3.58\times10^{-13}$ relative error in 22.59 seconds; a small conic comparison
checks the resulting solver. These are numerical checks, not speedup or
usefulness findings. A declared single follow-up waits for the original fit and
runs only if its final relative surrogate gap exceeds 0.005, with the same
starting weights and iteration budget. Both outcomes will be retained.
