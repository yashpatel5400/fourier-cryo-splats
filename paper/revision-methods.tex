\section{Development After Independent Review}\label{sec:revision}
The original frozen protocols and their outcomes are unchanged. This section
reports subsequent development, including negative experimental sensitivity
results. It does not establish that the current method meets the usefulness
requirements raised by the independent review.
The old frozen evaluators abort on detected undercoverage before retaining the
offending record. Neither completed study encountered that branch, but the
failure policy was flawed. Their source remains reproducible; new diagnostic
evaluators persist results before flagging an audit failure.

\subsection{Matrix-free shared fields and a numerical failure budget}
Each first or second pose-derivative field is a Fourier sum multiplied by one of
ten monomials $1,x,y,z,x^2,y^2,z^2,xy,xz,yz$. Summing coefficient combinations
before transforming gives a field/adjoint pair with ten type-3 NUFFTs per
direction. No global particle-by-particle field Gram is formed. Positive group
scales $d_j$ may come from streamed block norms or from polynomial coefficient
envelopes. They need not equal exact block norms. Identically zero blocks may
be omitted; blocks that can become nonzero during optimization require positive
scales. Particle-specific pose radii are accepted.

An approximate leading eigenvalue is insufficient for an upper audit. Random
starts have a long history in spectral estimation \citep{kw1992}; the following
elementary construction states our particular numerical probability budget.
For a fixed positive semidefinite $C=FF^\top$, draw $m$ independent standard
Gaussian probes $g_j$, independently of the estimator-selection process. Set
$c=\sqrt{2}\,\operatorname{erf}^{-1}(\delta^{1/m})$. Fix a leading unit
eigenvector $v$ before the draws. Since
\[
 \Pr\{\max_j|v^\top g_j|<c\}=\delta,
\]
with probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for every integer $p\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_{\max}(C)
 \le \left(\frac{\max_j\norm{C^p g_j}}{c}\right)^{1/p}.
 \label{eq:randompower}
\end{equation}
The proof retains the leading eigenvector component of $C^p g_j$ in its norm.
Stable normalization tracks the product of norms in log space. The minimum
across powers is valid on the same event; a deterministic trace bound may cap
it. A Rayleigh value is recorded only as a lower diagnostic.

Add the analytic integration pad to this spectral upper value before using the
shared-field inequality. Allocate $\alpha_{\rm noise}+\delta\le\alpha$. A union
bound then includes numerical randomization in the stated failure budget.
Candidate selection using the same probes is not licensed: weights and scales
must be fixed before fresh final probes, or all candidates need a simultaneous
budget. Operator roundoff and NUFFT approximation remain numerical checks,
not validated arithmetic.

Completed radius-five audits with 128 and 1,024 particles take 50.3 and 353.3
seconds under concurrent Mac load, with measured peak resident memory of 390
and 434 MB. Their relative half-widths are 0.438 and 0.740, respectively. They
use different fitted estimators, so the latter result does not measure a
particle-count benefit. The 128-particle randomized field bound is 1.157 times
the dense comparator. A distinct 10,000-particle, radius-12 audit with 220
independent Fourier pairs per particle completes in 4,965 seconds with 1.95 GB
peak resident memory. Its randomized eigenvalue upper is 1.136 times the
Rayleigh lower diagnostic. Relative half-width is 0.655 at $1^\circ$ and
0.5\,\AA\ translation. This uses deterministic matched-filter weights for a
broad target, not optimized reconstruction weights. It establishes numerical
scale under supplied simulation bounds, not useful fine-feature inference.

\subsection{Including pose uncertainty in estimator optimization}
Fix the group scales, let $F(w)$ be the scaled quadrature field matrix, and let
$a_q(w)$ be each real/imaginary weight pair's Euclidean amplitude. A nonnegative
matrix $M$ maps these amplitudes to polynomial coefficient envelopes. With
integration constant $E$, $R=B+P$, and integrated cubic coefficients $r_q\ge0$,
the revised sum objective is
\begin{align}
 J(w)&=z\norm{w}+B\norm{\ell-\A^*w}+p(w)
       +\sum_q r_q a_q(w),\nonumber\\
 p(w)&=R\sqrt{\sum_jd_j}
 \sqrt{\norm{F(w)}_{\rm op}^2+E\norm{Ma(w)}^2}.
 \label{eq:poseoptimized}
\end{align}
This is convex: $F$ is linear, $M$ is nonnegative, and the Euclidean norm is
monotone on the nonnegative orthant. It is an upper objective for the prescribed
class, not the exact minimax nonlinear-pose risk. Approximate singular modes
and log-sum-exp smoothing guide numerical optimization. The final primal upper
audit uses Equation~\eqref{eq:randompower} with fresh probes.

A feasible dual lower bound remains available without an optimizer-success
claim. Choose $v\in H$ and supporting linear functionals $g_p,g_r$ such that
\begin{align*}
 \norm{v}&\le B,&\norm{\A v-g_p-g_r}&\le z,\\
 g_p^\top x&\le p(x),&
 g_r^\top x&\le\sum_qr_q a_q(x)\quad\text{for all }x.
\end{align*}
The four support inequalities in Equation~\eqref{eq:poseoptimized} yield
$J(x)\ge\ip{v}{\ell}$ by cancellation. Unit singular-direction outer products
and their convex mixtures have nuclear norm at most one, giving valid spectral
supports even when the selected modes are not leading. Coefficient and cubic
norm supports are combined with them. A padded continuous Gram-action error
bounds the remaining dual constraint; scaling all three dual quantities by
$\min(1,z/D)$ for an upper bound $D$ makes it feasible. The implementation
details and full support argument are recorded with the source.

An independently assembled small CLARABEL conic instance brackets its optimum
with relative gap $2.60\times10^{-4}$. The first larger, 20-iteration development
solve reduces a one-degree interval to 0.255 of no data but has a 97.8\% gap;
it is not converged. Six subsequent feasible nonlinear searches reach 0.432 of
its bias upper bound, and minimum reference sign power is below $10^{-100}$.
The first completed 100-iteration case in the three-stack grid, 10049 at
$0.5^\circ$ and 0.5\,\AA\ translation, has relative width 0.221 and a 25.3\%
gap. Feasible searches reach 0.608 of its bias upper, but minimum reference
sign power is only $8.44\times10^{-5}$. Its completed one-degree counterpart
has width 0.336, an 85.3\% gap and minimum reference power $7.07\times10^{-25}$.
A feasible convex mixture of twelve spectral dual supports reduces the gap
only to 85.1\%, without changing the estimator or interval.

The original 10028 half-degree run failed when only seven of eight requested
eigenvectors converged; the failure and saved weights are retained. Checkpointed
continuations use an isolated two-thread CPU runtime with unchanged block
scales and fresh final spectral probes. Available partial modes can guide the
approximate optimizer, but are never used as upper bounds. The completed
10076 half-degree continuation has width 0.161, a 5.0\% gap, minimum reference
sign power 0.670, and feasible stress bias reaching 0.650 of the upper bound.
This is a broad-target conditional result; the remaining grid is in progress.
These intermediate outcomes do not establish broad practical usefulness.

\paragraph{Nonsmooth optimization and dual diagnostics.}
The longer legacy one-degree smooth probe fails before its final audit when
only seven of eight modes converge; it has no certified result. To separate
optimizer failure from estimator quality, we also restrict weights to $w=Vb$
and exchange spectral supporting cuts. For unit $u,v$,
$g^\top w=v^\top F(w)u\le\norm{F(w)}_{\rm op}$.
Noise, density-residual and cubic pair norms then form a small conic problem
with constraints $t\ge g_j^\top Vb$. The unknown density class remains
continuous. The restricted cut value is an optimization device, not a
full-space lower bound; integration padding and fresh spectral probes remain
in the final audit.

Conic cut multipliers obey $\mu_j\ge0$ and $\sum_j\mu_j\le
R\sqrt{\sum d}$, so $\sum_j\mu_jg_j$ is a valid full-space pose dual
support. Cubic cone duals, projected onto their known pair balls, avoid
normalizing nearly zero weight pairs. More generally, minimizing the dual
constraint defect over a convex hull of pose supports plus these pair balls
can improve a lower bound without changing any interval. An independent
small full spectral-norm conic problem checks the cut solver and support
inequalities, including a nonzero optimum and an active zero pair.

On 10049 at two degrees and 0.5\,\AA, the original completed fit has relative
width 0.761 and a 99.3\% gap. Joint dual postprocessing reduces that gap to
63.1\% with unchanged weights. Two consecutive thirty-round restricted-cut
probes reduce width to 0.692 and 0.689. Their final local feasible stress
biases reach 0.459 and 0.466 of the respective upper bounds; reference sign
power remains below $10^{-240}$. The follow-up's own joint dual gap is still
93.9\%. These limited subspaces and dual directions do not establish full-space
optimality or useful detection at two degrees.

A six-round conic trial optimizes all observation weights, projecting only
the inner density residual onto a target-anchored continuous Hilbert basis.
The projected residual is a lower relaxation, while the final audit retains
the full density class. At rank 256 the new candidates have much larger
ambient objectives than the input; the retained weights are unchanged.
A fresh audit gives width 0.690 and gap 76.2\%. A thirty-round adaptive
continuous residual-enrichment follow-up takes 721 seconds and raises the
valid lower bound to 9.200, reducing the original sum-objective gap to
18.2\%, again with unchanged weights. This gap concerns the original triangle
objective, not the subsequent tighter cross-term bound. These diagnostics
distinguish improved lower bounds from an improved reconstruction.

A sixty-round full-weight continuation, with the latest eight candidates
averaged before independent evaluation, changes the retained weights. It takes
2,330 seconds, gives a sum-objective upper bound 10.523 and feasible lower
bound 9.845 (6.44\% gap), and reduces the relative width to 0.646. The
strongest saved feasible nonlinear bias reaches 0.521 of its upper bound.
This establishes progress on the original objective, not useful reference
sign detection or optimality of the subsequent joint expression.

\paragraph{Retaining the density/pose cross term.}
Let $h=\ell-A_0^*w$ and write the existing nonlinear adjoint perturbation as
$D=Fv+e$, where $\norm v\le L=\sqrt{\sum d}$ and $\norm e\le r$.
Here each particle lies in the joint scaled five-dimensional unit ball.
Simultaneous maximal rotation and translation are excluded. For their product
set, replace $L^2$ by $2\sum_i d_i^{(1)}+4\sum_i d_i^{(2)}$ and scale $f$
by the corresponding radius ratio. The separate phase bounds in the cubic
remainder already cover either set. The two nuisance classes are reported
separately.
Suppose $f$ bounds $\norm{Fv}$ and $c$ bounds $\norm{F^*h}$.
The scalar bias for $\rho=\rho_0+u$ is
$\langle u,h-D\rangle-\langle\rho_0,D\rangle$. Thus
\begin{equation}
 b_{\rm joint}=B\sqrt{\norm h^2+2Lc+f^2}+Pf+(B+P)r
 \label{eq:joint-cross-bias}
\end{equation}
is an upper bound: expand $\norm{h-Fv}^2$, bound the cross term by $Lc$,
and apply Cauchy--Schwarz to $u$ and $\rho_0$. Take the minimum with the
preceding triangle bound on the same spectral event. This elementary
inequality is a post-audit refinement, not a new general theorem or a
claim that the expression is convex in $w$.

Gaussian target moments evaluate $\langle\ell,F_j\rangle$ analytically
on the cube, including boundary terms. For
$I_j=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}x^jg_{\mu,s}(x)e^{i\omega x}\,dx$,
integration by parts gives
\begin{align*}
 I_1&=(\mu+is^2\omega)I_0-s^2[g_{\mu,s}(x)e^{i\omega x}]_{-1/2}^{1/2},\\
 I_2&=(\mu+is^2\omega)I_1+s^2I_0
       -s^2[xg_{\mu,s}(x)e^{i\omega x}]_{-1/2}^{1/2}.
\end{align*}
For the finite Fourier-polynomial product $\langle A_0^*w,F_j\rangle$,
the existing quadrature error gives a cross-vector norm pad $ET\norm m$,
where $T$ is the absolute Fourier coefficient sum and $m$ contains the
scaled column coefficient bounds. Ordinary numerical roundoff remains
subject to the preceding caveat.

\subsection{An experimental noise-calibration attempt}
The following elementary conservative calculation allows nonzero calibration
signals. Suppose independent $Y_i\sim N(\mu_i,\Sigma)$ have arbitrary fixed
means and a common covariance. Put $T=\operatorname{tr}\Sigma$ and
$S=\sum_i\norm{Y_i}^2$. For $\lambda>0$,
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb E e^{-\lambda S}
 &\le\det(I+2\lambda\Sigma)^{-n/2}\\
 &\le(1+2\lambda T)^{-n/2}.
\end{align*}
The first inequality follows from the noncentral Gaussian Laplace transform;
the second uses $\prod_j(1+x_j)\ge1+\sum_jx_j$ for nonnegative eigenvalues.
Optimizing the exponential Markov inequality gives
\[
 \Pr\{S\le ntT\}\le
 \exp\{n(1-t+\log t)/2\},\qquad 0<t<1.
\]
Choose $t$ to make the right side $\beta$. Then $T_+=S/(nt)$ is an upper bound
on $T$ with probability at least $1-\beta$, and $\Sigma\preceq T_+I$ on that
event. This can be much more conservative than estimating a known diagonal
noise model. Independent inference with the same covariance permits allocating
$\alpha_{\rm noise}+\beta\le\alpha$. The result does not prove the covariance
commonality or pose-independence assumptions for archive data.

The exploratory three-stack attempt fits each old pilot's scalar amplitude on
pilot-pool particles, then calibrates from one particle per new exposure group.
Half of the new groups calibrate, and the others supply energy diagnostics.
Inference also uses one particle per old inference exposure group. No background
mask or fitted-signal subtraction enters the calibration statistic. We use
$\beta=0.005$, $\alpha_{\rm noise}=0.045$, density radius two, and prescribed
0/1/2-degree sensitivities with 0.5\,\AA\ shifts at nonzero angles.

\paragraph{Independent calibration in the estimator's directions.}
The trace envelope protects every direction, although a fixed feature estimator
needs only one scalar variance. Let $V$ contain its raw-coordinate particle
weight rows $v_i^\top$, chosen independently of a second calibration pool.
Under the same common-covariance and independence assumptions, its noise
variance is $s^2=\sum_i v_i^\top\Sigma v_i=\operatorname{tr}(V\Sigma V^\top)$.
Applying the preceding lemma to $VY_j$ gives the following direct corollary:
\[
 s^2\le s_+^2:=\frac{\sum_j\norm{VY_j}^2}{nt}
 \quad\text{with probability at least }1-\beta.
\]
The projected coordinates may be correlated. Combining this event with the
bias-aware critical width at $\alpha_{\rm noise}$ gives pointwise failure at
most $\beta+\alpha_{\rm noise}$. Indeed, the width is at least the bias bound,
and its Gaussian outside probability increases with noise scale. This is an
elementary specialization of the trace argument, not a new concentration
principle or a simultaneous guarantee for selected targets.

For the same calibration pool, $\sum_j\norm{VY_j}^2\le
\norm{V}_F^2\sum_j\norm{Y_j}^2$, so directional calibration cannot exceed
the original trace envelope for these weights. To preserve independence here,
we retain weights selected using the original first exposure half and calibrate
their variance with the second half. The pools therefore differ in the empirical
comparison. Before projection, weights are pulled back through each particle's
known centering phase: complex coefficient $w_R+iw_I$ becomes
$\overline p(w_R+iw_I)/\sigma_{\rm old}$ in raw Fourier coordinates.
Ignoring this step would be incorrect for colored noise.

\paragraph{Refitting with an independent covariance guide.}
We also use the first exposure half's uncentered second moment $\widehat C$
to form $C=0.8\widehat C+0.2\operatorname{tr}(\widehat C)I/m$; the second half
still supplies the final directional noise bound. This positive matrix guides
optimization only. With known centering matrices $O_i$, set
$W=\operatorname{diag}_i[(O_iCO_i^\top)^{-1/2}]$ and write $w=Wx$.
The continuous fixed-pose problem then uses target vector $W\A\ell$ and Gram

Quadrature residual errors are evaluated at $Wx$; transformed Gram-action
errors multiply by $\norm W$. None of these proxy quantities replaces the

\subsection{Uniform CTF sensitivities}
For fixed voltage, spherical aberration and amplitude contrast $a$,
$C=\sin(\gamma-\arcsin a)$. If each defocus axis can change by $d$ and its
astigmatism angle by $b$ radians, a conservative defocus error is
$d+|\mathrm{df}_U-\mathrm{df}_V|\sin(\min(b,\pi/2))$. Multiplying by
$\pi\lambda s^2$ and adding the phase-shift radius bounds the phase error.
Evaluate the exact sine range on that interval, then multiply by the positive
relative gain/B-factor range. This yields an absolute transfer envelope
$e_{iq}$ without division by a CTF zero.

For any allowed pose, a complex Fourier wave has unit modulus on the unit-volume
cube. Therefore the additive CTF bias is bounded by
\begin{equation}
 b_{\rm CTF}\le (B+P)\sum_{iq}
       |w_{iq,\mathbb C}|e_{iq}/\sigma.
\end{equation}
This term can be combined with the nominal-CTF pose bound, including simultaneous
errors, but discards cancellations and is not optimized jointly with them.
Table~\ref{tab:revisionctf} applies it to all six saved radius-12 fixed-pose
fine-feature fits. Defocus uncertainty of 100\,\AA\ already causes two no-data
fallbacks; all six fall back at 500\,\AA\ and under the combined sensitivities.
These negative results diagnose a loose audit, not an impossibility theorem for
other estimators or a calibrated distribution of experimental CTF error.

\subsection{An explicit support ablation}
Changing support changes the density class. For a centered cube of side
$s\le1$, the transformation $\widetilde\rho(u)=s^{3/2}\rho(su)$ is an $L^2$
isometry. The transformed acquisition uses $k'=sk$, $q'=sq$, $C'=s^{3/2}C$
and translation radius $t'=t/s$. A normalized Gaussian target uses center
$c/s$, width $\sigma_\ell/s$ and amplitude $s^{-3/2}$. Thus the target and
nonlinear acquisition values are preserved, including physical translations.

Hard support need not be imposed without a sensitivity allowance. Write
$\rho=p_S+d_S+r_{S^c}$, where $p_S,d_S$ are supported inside the smaller cube,
$\norm{d_S}\le B$ and $\norm{r_{S^c}}\le\epsilon$. Add to the inside audit
\begin{align}
 b_{\rm tail}&\le\epsilon\left[\norm{\A_0^*w-\ell}
               +\sum_{iq}|c_{iq}|d_{iq}\right],\nonumber\\
 d_{iq}&=\min\!\left(2,2\pi\sqrt{
    \theta^2\norm{k_{iq}}^2/12+t^2\norm{q_{iq}}^2}\right).
\end{align}
Indeed, the spatial phase has zero mean on the cube, its integrated squared
linear part is $\norm{\Delta k}^2/12$, and the rotation chord is at most
$\theta\norm{k}$. Apply $|e^{ia}-e^{ib}|\le\min(2,|a-b|)$ and the triangle
inequality, then restrict the residual to $S^c$. The bound is conservative;
it does not infer $\epsilon$ from the data.

The initial radius-12, 10\,\AA\-sigma support probe on 10049 uses 128 particles,
$1^\circ$ and 0.5\,\AA\ translation. Side $s=0.5$ gives relative width 0.417
under hard support, yet the cropped-reference minimum sign power is below
$10^{-150}$. A tail radius of 0.1 causes no-data fallback. The original
unit-norm reference has outside norm 0.320 and is excluded by all these tail
settings; it is not relabeled as an in-class validation example. The completed
full-cube control takes 6,324 seconds, attains a fixed-pose sum-objective gap
of 0.19\%, and falls back to no data at every tested tail allowance after
adding pose sensitivity. Its integrated cubic term alone is 115.4 in the
normalized density units. A converged nominal fit therefore does not establish
a useful pose-aware interval.

\subsection{A closer Fourier variational comparison}
We independently implement the fixed-pose Gaussian variational objective of
\citet{ullrich2020}, Equation~14, in a Hermitian Fourier grid with trilinear
slice interpolation. The original authors' full software and pose-learning
pipeline are not reproduced. CTFs, an independent pilot as prior mean, physical
target normalization and a declared prior-scale sweep are explicit extensions.
For $x\sim N(m_0,\tau^2I)$ and whitened $y=Ax+e$, put
$Q=A^\top A+\tau^{-2}I$. The diagonal-Gaussian optimum has the full posterior
mean and coordinate variances $1/Q_{jj}$. A target's full posterior variance
is $\ell^\top Q^{-1}\ell$, whereas its diagonal approximation is
$\sum_j\ell_j^2/Q_{jj}$. Differentiating the Gaussian objective verifies these
solutions without stochastic-training error. The identity
\[
 \norm{w}^2+\tau^2\norm{A^\top w-\ell}^2
   =\ell^\top Q^{-1}\ell,\qquad w=AQ^{-1}\ell,
\]
separately checks prior-predictive calibration.

Each stack uses 1,024 particles, radius 12 and two 10\,\AA\-sigma targets:
a central average and a contrast between centers at $z=\pm10$\,\AA.
The prior expected squared deviation norms are 0.25, 1 and 4; all settings
are retained. The sparse conditional calculations take 3.3--3.9 seconds per
stack and 45.0 MB per acquisition matrix. The full posterior's matched
prior-predictive coverage is 0.95, and the variance identity error is below
$4\times10^{-16}$. Diagonal feature intervals are \emph{wider} in every tested
case, by factors 1.073--1.415, with matched prior-predictive coverage
0.964--0.994. Diagonal covariance is therefore not a general explanation for
undercoverage of these targets.

Table~\ref{tab:fouriervi} and Figure~\ref{fig:fouriervi} instead report
fixed-map, known-noise coverage. Interpolation and finite-representation forward
discrepancy is 16.8\%, 16.2\% and 25.3\% across the three reference generators.
Together with prior shrinkage, this is a material baseline limitation. These
controls do not refute Bayesian prior-predictive guarantees or constitute
experimental density-coverage measurements. All matched-model and nonlinear
pose controls are retained in the public records.

We also audit each of these 18 estimators in the continuous cube without
changing its original affine center. Let $\kappa$ be the known difference
between its interpolated-prior center and the exact continuous-pilot center.
The fixed-pose bias bound is then $|\kappa|+2\norm{\ell-\A^*w}$. Audited
half-widths are 0.257--0.815 of no data and 56--352 times the original full
posterior widths. The nominal continuous-map checks are covered conservatively,
but sign power is numerically zero throughout, even after correcting
$\kappa$. Thus the broad continuous class already loses useful precision before
adding pose uncertainty. This does not establish impossibility for a physically
narrower class or a different estimator.
