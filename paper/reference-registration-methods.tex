\section{Reference Registration and Completed RELION Baselines}
\label{sec:referenceframe}
The first completed unknown-pose RELION continuation on 10028 reports
convergence and preserves the exposure half labels. It takes 5,492 seconds for
the remaining pilot-only initializer and 4,012 seconds for auto-refinement.
Native half-map FSC crosses 0.5 at 21.21\,\AA; its 0.143 crossing is censored at
the sampled 16.08\,\AA\ limit. RELION joins halves below $1/40$\,\AA$^{-1}$,
which must be distinguished from independent high-frequency agreement.
On 10049, the remaining initializer takes 4,547 seconds and refinement 921
seconds. It reports convergence with verified exposure halves, but is weaker:
native half FSC crosses 0.5/0.143 at 22.73/18.95\,\AA. Registered-reference
FSC crosses at 45.43/19.85\,\AA; its mean is only 0.202. Local angular and
translation accuracy estimates are 8.87 degrees and 4.579\,\AA, not calibrated
uniform pose bounds. Convergence does not establish correct reconstruction.
The 10076 continuation remains in progress.

Initially, every method's deposited-map FSC on 10028 is weak despite strong
cross-method agreement. This motivates a separately declared frame audit,
retaining every original comparison. Register each deposited map to the old
independent Gaussian pilot only, using the same fixed 48-start rigid/hand search
at 40\,\AA\ lowpass. All three transforms are committed before their subsequent
reconstruction comparisons. Neither inference-half maps, interval outcomes nor
reference FSC select a transform. This is post-outcome development with a local
search, not fresh confirmation or guaranteed global registration.

These results show that an unverified reference frame can dominate a comparison.
All eleven completed method/stack cases and both reference curves are retained.
Reference agreement remains affected by pilot selection, interpolation,
preprocessing and partial data dependence, and is not independent accuracy or
density coverage. Historical reference-based uncertainty simulations keep their
original unregistered generators and numerical outcomes. They are conditional
implementation studies, not evidence that those simulated features were in the
correct biological location of the consensus reconstruction.

\paragraph{Fixed-estimator target sensitivity.}
A subsequent protocol retains all twelve locked 20\,\AA-SD targets, their
weights, noise levels, bias bounds and fallback rules. All original nominal
reference centers replay before changing the generator to the registered
64-cube reference with unit constant-cell $L^2$ norm. All 120 conditional
target/pose checks are retained. Fixed-pose sign powers are at least 0.9563;
every original one-/two-degree fixed-weight audit remains uninformative.
Recomputing only the reference targets, with their amplitude fitted on the
old pilot pool, leaves all 48 experimental intervals unchanged and still
containing the approximate reference. This is sensitivity to a comparison
frame, not improved estimation or density calibration.

On the already selected 10049 region 1, minimum simulated sign power across
the three declared poses changes from 0.00654 to 0.95725 for the original
cubic estimator, from $7.73\times10^{-7}$ to 0.26042 for the coordinate design,
and from 0.00663 to 0.95770 for the reduced-subspace design. Their widths do
not change. Original and reduced-subspace weights are identical to numerical
precision; separate numerical audits explain their small width difference.
The larger power results from the generator's frame and does not resolve the
optimization gap, the 20\,\AA\ target scale, or experimental input conditions.

\paragraph{Experimental cubic application.}
Another declared protocol then applies all three completed cubic estimators
to the original inference images using the previously collected 128
noise-calibration exposure representatives. It replays the published fixed
estimator's observed center, calibrated standard deviation and half-width
before computing new outcomes. The group-noise bound permits arbitrary
within-exposure dependence: these 128 inference particles occupy only 26
exposures, with maximum group size twelve. The Gaussian tail, calibration
and spectral allowances are $(.045-10^{-6})/12$, $.005/12$ and $10^{-6}/12$ per
procedure. Under the required fixed-design assumptions, a union bound for
these three alternatives is $.0125$; inspected-data reuse precludes treating
this calculation as fresh experimental coverage validation.

The original cubic center is 2.46178 and its half-width is 4.30107 (0.35374
of no data). The coordinate design gives $2.48844\pm4.82603$ (0.39692), and
the reduced-subspace design gives $2.46178\pm4.29998$ (0.35365). None excludes
zero or uses the no-data fallback. All contain the registered approximate
target 2.48846 and the old-frame target 0.71012. The original cubic noise SD
upper is 1.01002, 4.55146 times its supplied simulation SD. These results
locate a practical obstacle: useful power under supplied noise does not
transfer to this experimental noise bound. Reference inclusion is only
agreement, and the underlying pose, covariance and density-class conditions
remain unverified. Both protocols, all alternatives and exact arrays are kept.

\paragraph{Centered calibration with arbitrary means.}
The uncentered SD bound includes signal energy. A separately declared follow-up
uses fixed orthogonal contrasts to remove a common component. If independent
rows satisfy $Y_i\sim N(\mu_i,\Sigma)$ with arbitrary fixed means, choose
$Q\in\mathbb R^{(n-1)\times n}$ with $QQ^\top=I$ and $Q\mathbf1=0$.
Rows of $QY$ are independent Gaussian vectors with covariance $\Sigma$:
their cross-covariance is $(QQ^\top)_{ij}\Sigma$. Their means need not vanish.
The previous noncentral trace bound therefore applies with $n-1$ rows, and
$\|QY\|_F^2=\sum_i\|Y_i-\bar Y\|^2$. Apply the same contrast to the
group-inflated weight projections. This is a classical Gaussian orthogonal-
contrast argument, not a new general coverage principle. The matrix $Q$ is
fixed; an image-fitted contrast would need separate justification.

All uncentered SD bounds replay before this follow-up. On the same inspected
calibration cohorts, centered/uncentered SD ratios are 0.437--0.823 across all
twelve fixed estimators. Fixed-pose zero exclusions increase from six to ten
and shift-only exclusions from four to six; rotational exclusions remain zero
and all sixteen no-data fallbacks remain. Five registered reference targets
now lie outside: 10076 regions 1 and 3 at fixed/shift-only poses, and its
matched center at fixed poses. The other 43 fixed-weight intervals contain
the reference. This is disagreement with one Class A assembly state, not
a ground-truth coverage label for the heterogeneous population. Original, coordinate and reduced-subspace cubic intervals
become $2.46178\pm3.07893$, $2.48844\pm3.60408$ and $2.46178\pm3.07784$,
respectively. None excludes zero. For the original cubic fit, the SD upper
falls from 1.01002 to 0.55294, still exceeding the supplied simulation SD
0.22191. A smaller bound does not identify pure noise or establish the
experimental assumptions. The two procedures are separately reported
alternatives; their unadjusted minimum is never used. These outcomes motivate
the separately declared, still-running adaptive subspace-enrichment study;
that ongoing fit has no result in this revision.


\paragraph{A finite family of projected calibration designs.}
A further declared diagnostic compares seven noise-independent orthogonal
contrasts: no projection, mean removal, two CTF-design spans and three
pilot-prediction spans. Each bound receives $\beta/7$, so their minimum
retains the original total allowance by a union bound under the same Gaussian
and fixed-design premises. Every contrast is retained. Selected SD bounds
are 0.981--1.030 times the separately reported centered bounds: four fixed
estimators improve and eight worsen. Exclusion counts and the five Class A
reference disagreements are unchanged. Cubic half-widths are 3.11643,
3.64233 and 3.11534, all slightly worse than centering alone. This diagnostic
does not improve experimental conclusions. Upstream metadata independence
remains unverified, and no unadjusted minimum across historical procedures
is used.
