## Identities that check out

**SO(3) convention.** The phase is 2π k·exp(tW)x + 2πt s q·τ with W = a[u]×, row-vector action. The first-derivative coefficient in `_phase_coefficients` is 2πa k×e_a, and kW = k×u for the standard skew matrix, so the linear term is consistent. The curvature helper implements kW² = (k·u)u − ||u||²k with the symmetric-Hessian split (0.5 k_a e_b + 0.5 k_b e_a, minus k on the diagonal). In `_cubic_coefficients` the degree-2 monomial coefficients absorb both the Taylor 1/2 and monomial multiplicity through `factor`, which is correct for both diagonal and off-diagonal monomials. The forward check against `Rotation.from_rotvec` in the error-order test is what pins the convention to the reference projections, since the halving ratios near 16 would collapse to about 2 under a wrong sign.

**Φ3 and the exponential coefficients.** For skew W with axis u, W³ = −||u||²W, so 2πkW³x/6 = −(a²||u_rot||²/6)Φ1_rot. The code adds −i a² b_a/6 to exactly the monomials u_a u_c² for each (a,c), which enumerates Σ_c u_c² Σ_a u_a b_a once per monomial. E3 = iΦ3 − Φ1Φ2 − iΦ1³/6 follows from the exponential series, and the Φ1³/6 coefficient of u^α is b^α/α!, matching the division by the product of factorials. Truncating spatial products at degree 3 loses nothing because Φ1³ has degree 3 and Φ1Φ2 degree 2.

**Normalized lifts and radius.** Σ_{|α|=m}(m!/α!)u^{2α} = ||u||^{2m} is the multinomial theorem. Dividing the coefficient tensor by `NORMALIZATION` converts monomial coefficients to lift coordinates. With column denominators sqrt(d_im), the vector actually fed to the raw operator is sqrt(d_im) z_im, so ||v||² ≤ Σ d_im under per-particle joint unit balls. The block layout in `_to_particle_columns` and the `np.repeat` pattern in `establish_quadrature_design_scaling` agree (degree-major, particle-major inside). Zero sampled blocks get scale 1 and are still counted in L, which is conservative but valid.

**Quadratic restriction.** With cubic lifts zero, the degree-2 column equals the old half-Hessian column times sqrt2 off-diagonal, and the test confirms this to 2e-14. Note both operators share `_curvature`, so this test does not independently verify curvature. The scalar Taylor recurrence test does.

**Degree-six pad.** Real Gram entries expand as 0.5 Re(f_a f_b + f_a conj f_b), frequencies k_q ± k_q' within 2 max|k_a|, monomial degree at most 6. The Peano kernel of Gauss quadrature is nonnegative, so the constant applies to complex integrands without an extra factor, and the tensor error is a sum of per-axis errors because the other axes have positive unit-sum weights and |x_b|^β ≤ 1. The rank-one majorant then gives λ(G_cont) ≤ λ(G_quad) + ε||m||². The cross pad ε Σ|c| ||m|| in `cubic_residual_cross` uses degree 6 where degree 3 suffices, which is only conservative.

**Cell and Gaussian moments.** The Legendre reductions s² = (2P_2 + P_0)/3 and s³ = (2P_3 + 3P_1)/5 with (1/2)∫P_n e^{izs}ds = iⁿj_n(z) give the four listed formulas, with parity handled for negative arguments. The type-2 phase shift exp(iπk/box) correctly moves the grid origin to the half-cell offset. The Gaussian recurrence m_n = (μ + iσ²ω)m_{n−1} + (n−1)σ²m_{n−2} − σ²(boundary terms) is the integration-by-parts identity with the correct boundary signs.

**Bell remainder.** `partial_bell` implements the standard recurrence and the fourth-order test values (7, 67, 72, 16) are right. The chain-rule bound Σ_j B_{k,j}(M)S_j uses uniform path bounds, Frobenius contraction, and a determinant-one change of variables into the expanded cube. The joint-ball speed via Cauchy–Schwarz on (||u_rot||, ||u_tr||) is correct. The Sobolev identity with (−1)^m from i^{2m} is correct and independently tested against spherical and tensor quadrature. The embedding residual pad follows from Leibniz on (e^{itδ}−1)M(t) with |e^{itδ}−1| ≤ |δ| and complete Bell bounds on M^{(r)}. Importantly, the operator expands the exact phase with q·τ, and the remainder bounds the fourth derivative of that same function, so the two are consistent.

**Random spectral certificate.** The threshold c = sqrt2 erfinv(δ^{1/m}) gives P(all m probes have |⟨v_max,g⟩| < c) = δ, and the simultaneous-in-p upper bound is deterministic on that event. The union with α_noise is a plain union bound. This is valid in real arithmetic for a fixed PSD matrix.

## Issues

**C1. Gaussian-moment cancellation is untested in the audit regime.** Severity: medium. Location: `gaussian_moments_cubic` and `gaussian_fourier_moments`, the `zero` term, tested only in `test_gaussian_twenty_moments_independent_tensor_quadrature` at σ = 0.13, |k| ≲ 3. The bracket Φ(a − ib) − Φ(c − ib) with b = σω, a = (0.5 − μ)/σ has individual terms of size e^{(b² − a²)/2} while the true product is of order 1. When σ²ω exceeds 0.5 − |μ| the relative error grows like eps·e^{(b²−a²)/2}, silently, and only overflows to a raised error at the extreme. At frequency radius 12 and a 20 Å target this depends on the field size, which the packet does not state. Fix: add a test at the actual (σ, μ, ||k|| = 12) values against 200-node tensor Gauss, and assert a relative tolerance of 1e-10. If the pilot centers lie near a cube face, this becomes a real correctness risk, not a conservative one.

**C2. Freshness of seed 640001 is unverifiable and dimension-shared.** Severity: potentially high, currently unknown. Location: `CONFIG['certificate_seed']` in the audit script and the declaration. Both audits use order 80, so both draw probes in the same 512,000-dimensional space. If the quadratic audit for this case used the same seed, the probes are byte-identical, and the cubic matrix was constructed after observing the quadratic result that depended on those probes. That breaks the "fixed matrix, then fresh probes" hypothesis and the δ accounting. Falsification: read `certificate_seed` from the source audit JSON and every prior result under `pose-aware-*` for this dataset. If any equals 640001 at order 80, the certificate is not independent and must be redrawn with a new declared seed before results are read.

**C3. The proposition states a bound for an estimator it does not define.** Severity: low. Location: CUBIC-POSE-PROBE.md proof, second paragraph. The noiseless error also contains ⟨h, ρ0⟩, which is absent from the displayed bound. The bound is correct only for the pilot-corrected estimator that subtracts the known ⟨h, ρ0⟩, which is what the reference checks and the toy test assume via `pilot_target`. Additionally the script sets H = fit bias / B, which presupposes the fit's stored bias equals B times the continuous residual norm including its own quadrature pad. Fix: write the estimator explicitly in the proposition and add an assertion in the script that reconstructs fit bias from the stored residual norm and B.

**C4. The toy end-to-end bias test is weakly falsifying.** Severity: low. Location: `test_joint_continuous_bias_controls_sampled_nonlinear_fields`. The fixture draws k and q as independent Gaussians, so the least-squares embedding is meaningless and the embedding residual pad dominates the remainder. The remainder inequality and the final bias inequality are then satisfied with large slack, so an error in the main Bell term or in the Φ3 column could pass. The higher-remainder tests use planar q and are tighter, but they never exercise the cubic operator. Fix: add a planar-q variant of the fixture with small distortion, compute the ratio of bound to sampled remainder, and assert it is below a fixed factor such as 20. Also check the 4th-order scaling of the remainder directly.

**C5. Design scaling at order 12 is aliased.** Severity: low, conservative only. Location: `establish_quadrature_design_scaling`. Products of degree-3 polynomials with frequencies up to 24 cycles per field are nowhere near resolved by 12 Gauss nodes per axis, so the sampled block norms are noise-like. Any positive choice is valid, but poor scales inflate L·s. If the eventual pose field bound is much larger than the quadratic one at the same weights, this is the first suspect. Diagnostic: report the ratio of order-80 block traces to the design scales in the persisted output.

**C6. The spectral certificate has no floating-point margin.** Severity: low, already declared. Location: `gaussian_power_upper` applied to `op.spatial_gram` with NUFFT eps 1e-12. The quadratic path adds a roundoff pad in `grouped_gram_bounds`, but the matrix-free cubic path adds none. The packet says this explicitly. Suggested guard: report the adjoint residual |⟨v, Fu⟩ − ⟨F*v, u⟩| on the actual operator and include it as a heuristic pad, so the omission is visible in the output rather than silent.

**C7. `alpha_noise = alpha_total − δ` reuses the source's total while switching δ.** Severity: low. Location: audit script `result` dict. This is consistent with a stand-alone cubic interval only if the quadratic interval is not also reported for the same coverage claim. The script does not take the minimum of the two half-widths, so no unsafe reuse of the quadratic certificate is present in the code. The risk is downstream reporting. Fix: persist a flag that the cubic and quadratic intervals are alternatives, not jointly covering.

**No invalidity found** in the retained deterministic parts: lift norms, coefficient identities, pads, moment formulas, the Bell construction, and the joint bias inequality are correct as real-arithmetic statements. The distinctions are: C1 and C2 could be invalid depending on facts outside the packet; C3 is a statement gap; C4 through C7 are conservative or numerical-hygiene concerns.

## Test independence

The scalar Taylor recurrence test is independent of the operator's coefficient assembly and of `np.cross`, and the error-order check ties it to `Rotation.from_rotvec`. Adjoint and NUFFT-vs-direct tests are independent. The Sobolev tests use genuinely separate quadratures. The quadratic-restriction test shares `_curvature` with the base class and is therefore a consistency check, not a proof. The cell-moment test shares the box^1.5 normalization with its expected values, so it cannot detect a global normalization error; the script's assertion ||pilot|| = P covers that indirectly. The passing logs show 8 and 161 tests, which confirms nothing about the running empirical case.
